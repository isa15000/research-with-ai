What the data suggest

The highest estimate appears in southwest Washington, but the regional intervals overlap.

01

A notable spread

Among reportable regions, estimates range from 9.0% in Healthier Here to 15.9% in Cascade Pacific Action Alliance.

02

Not a league table

Wide, overlapping confidence intervals mean the apparent ordering may reflect sampling variation. The map identifies questions for further study.

03

A missing estimate is information

Thriving Together NCW is suppressed, and two other regions are flagged as statistically unreliable. Rural areas remain harder to characterize with survey data.

Data & methods

A regional snapshot, not a causal test

The analysis uses the latest common three-year regional period in the downloaded Washington Perinatal Dashboard file. Pooling 2021–2023 improves stability while retaining a recent view.

Health measure

Weighted Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS) estimates of postpartum depressive symptoms among respondents who recently gave birth. PRAMS is conducted by Washington DOH with CDC. These are survey estimates, not clinical diagnoses.

Study area and geography

Washington State, summarized into nine Accountable Communities of Health (ACH) regions. Public PRAMS geography is ACH rather than county to protect confidentiality and support adequate sample sizes.

Limitations

Symptoms are self-reported and may be affected by recall, response, or screening differences. Confidence intervals overlap. One estimate is suppressed and two are flagged as unreliable. Regional averages hide within-region variation, and this descriptive analysis cannot explain why differences occur. The source supports a mapped depression measure, not a comparable ACH-level combined depression-and-anxiety estimate.
